Behind your range

Part of every project is fixed. The rest depends on the size and what goes inside.

A 150 sq ft addition carries almost the same fixed investment as one three times the size: architectural drawings, structural engineering, energy calculations, the permit, and tying the new structure into your existing roof and foundation. Those items do not shrink with square footage, so the per-foot number climbs on smaller projects. The range assumes standard finishes; upgraded selections move the total up. Your exact investment comes from a site visit and a set of drawings.

How we calculate it. Every type of project starts with a fixed amount for drawings, engineering, permit preparation and site setup, plus a price per square foot that comes down as the space gets bigger. What goes inside (a bath, a kitchen, laundry hookups) is added on top. The figures come from Herrera Contractors projects completed in Seminole and Orange County at standard finishes, and we review them regularly. Figures last reviewed: October 2026.

What your investment includes

  • Walkthrough and measurements of the existing home
  • Conceptual design for your approval, then signed & sealed construction drawings with energy calculations
  • Building permit preparation and submission
  • Dumpster, portable restroom and site protection for the whole project
  • Foundation and slab raised to match your existing floor level
  • Block or framed walls, roof framing or engineered trusses, roof tied into the existing roof
  • Windows and exterior door per Florida Energy Code
  • Electrical: outlets, switches, lighting and ceiling fan per the approved plan
  • Plumbing: water and drain lines, fixtures and connection to the existing system per the approved plan (if applicable)
  • HVAC connection to your existing system, confirmed by the energy calculation during design
  • Insulation per energy calcs, drywall, texture, paint, baseboards and trim
  • Flooring allowance (vinyl plank or tile) with any upgrade handled as a change order
  • Final clean, walkthrough and workmanship warranty

Not included in the range

  • County or city impact fees, HOA fees and other third-party fees Always the owner's responsibility
  • Boundary survey Provided by the owner; we can order one for you
  • Landscaping, irrigation and exterior site work
  • Work inside the existing house beyond the connection points
  • Concealed conditions found during demolition Priced by written change order before we proceed

Questions people ask us

Straight answers before you call

How much does a 500 sq ft home addition cost in Central Florida?

As of October 2026, our investment range for a 500 sq ft ground-floor addition with no plumbing is $78,000 – $88,000 at standard finishes. A bath or a kitchen adds to that. Smaller additions start at $75,000, because drawings, engineering and permitting cost the same at any size. Use the estimator above for your own size and options.

How long does a home addition take?

About 28 weeks from signing to keys for a typical ground-floor addition: 6 weeks of design and drawings, 6 of permits and procurement, 8 of rough construction through the rough-in inspections, and 8 of finishes and final inspections. Conversions usually run shorter; ADU's and second stories longer.

Do I need a permit for an addition, a garage conversion or a Lanai enclosure?

Yes. In Seminole and Orange County every addition, garage conversion, Lanai enclosure, ADU and second story needs a building permit with signed and sealed drawings and an energy calculation. We prepare and submit the permit as part of every project.

Does a garage conversion add value to my house?

It turns unconditioned space into recorded living area, so you are increasing the value of the house and the equity you hold in it, usually for a lower investment than building new. The trade-off is the covered parking you give up, which matters to some buyers and some HOAs.

Can I build an ADU or in-law suite on my lot?

Many lots in Seminole and Orange County allow one, attached or detached, but it depends on zoning and on sewer and water tie-ins; we check both before drawings start. An ADU includes its own bath and kitchen, and it can house family or bring in rental income.
